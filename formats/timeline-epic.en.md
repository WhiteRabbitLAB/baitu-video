# Long history feature (id: timeline-epic)

中文:[timeline-epic.md](timeline-epic.md)

## In one line
Follow a very long timeline (hundreds to tens of thousands of years) and tell a whole field's history as one continuous, well-joined story; any length, aiming for the best result.

## Topic signals
- The field is visually rich (art history, architecture, writing, clothing, food).
- There's a thread that runs through the whole video (one motif, one question).
- The decision-maker wants "a narrated, continuous story with good joins", not chapters listed like a web page.

## Narrative skeleton (one art-history feature, 17′11″)
| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| Opening | The oldest starting point (check the latest research: the date of the oldest handprint was revised in 2026) |
| Chapters | Advance era by era, one representative work + one turning point per chapter |
| Global | Not only the West — civilizations in parallel |
| Close | Return to the thread that ran through it all |

## Required shots / through-line elements
- A chapter progress bar (mandatory for long videos); a permanent time scale.
- One continuous camera path (a long scroll, a fly-through); no jump cuts between chapters.

## Transition preferences
The camera only moves forward, travelling along the timeline; chapter boundaries use the change of style or scene itself as the transition.

## Fact and platform risks
- Check dates and attributions against the latest research; check the numbers in the title too (measured: the title's year figure was revised once to follow the latest research).
- Heavy rendering: estimate peak disk use before frame-by-frame rendering a long video, and delete each segment once rendered (a disk has filled up before).

## Suitable scenes
Done: three.js offline frame-by-frame rendering (headless Chromium needs Metal flags to use the GPU).

## Cases
- One art-history feature: landscape, 17′11″, 1.1 GB.
