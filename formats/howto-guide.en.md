# Scenario guide (id: howto-guide)

中文:[howto-guide.md](howto-guide.md)

## In one line
Around one concrete situation (a holiday trip, making slides, exam prep…), show step by step how to do it with a tool, where it goes wrong, and end with a checklist.

## Topic signals
- A timely moment (holidays, back to school, tax season) or a frequent pain point.
- The steps can be demonstrated; there are real pitfalls (the tool makes things up, information goes stale).

## Narrative skeleton (one finished video, 8 paragraphs, about 4′37″)
| Paragraph | Role |
|---|---|
| 1 | The pain point + the result first (what it looks like when done) |
| 2–5 | One step per paragraph: what goes in, what comes out, how to fix it |
| 6 | Pitfalls: where the tool goes wrong and how to check |
| 7 | "So how do you actually use it?" — the steps summarized |
| 8 | Checklist + a question for the comments |

## Required shots / through-line elements
- One "workbench" throughout (dialog box, terminal, map, itinerary).
- Each step's input and output side by side.

## Transition preferences
Push in on the part being worked on within the same workbench; the step number stays on screen.

## Fact and platform risks
- Specifics (prices, opening hours, policies) must be checked and dated; leave out anything uncertain.
- Domestic products may go unnamed; no URLs.
- Plenty of hooks so there's always a reason to keep watching (decision-maker's requirement).

## Suitable scenes
Validated: terminal-tech (one finished video). Suggested: field-notebook, info-cards.

## Cases
- One "plan a trip with AI" video: 8 paragraphs; acceptance caught a near-empty frame at 214 s, fixed with a big "So how do you actually use it?" title.
