# True-experiment story (id: experiment)

中文:[experiment.md](experiment.md)

## In one line
Tell one bizarre real experiment: how it was designed, what it found, why that's counter-intuitive, and what we can do with it.

## Topic signals
- Specific people, a specific year, a specific procedure ("the same food with two different labels", "a group volunteers to live on rations for half a year").
- A counter-intuitive result with clear limits (only a correlation / a small sample / no difference in self-report) — the limits are part of the drama.
- The best-received kind (measured on the author's nutrition channel); far more dramatic than "the story of nutrient X".

## Required shots / through-line elements
- **One "instrument" that runs through the whole video**: a monitor; weight-chart graph paper + a progress tag. The result paragraph is its full-screen moment.
- A timeline / flow chart explains the design in one go.
- No likenesses of real people: name tags, number cards, empty seats, wristbands.

## Transition preferences
"Paragraph-start pan" across the same desk / wall; zoom in and out of the through-line instrument.

## Fact and platform risks
- Separate "objective measurement" from "self-report"; correlation ≠ causation; state sample sizes for small studies.
- Curves with direction but no values ⇒ schematic shape + a permanent "schematic" label.
- Self-harm details stay out of the script (measured: the source material included a subject harming himself; not a word of it). Check viral numbers against primary sources.
