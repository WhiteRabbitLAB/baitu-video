# How a rule / version evolved (id: evolution)

中文:[evolution.md](evolution.md)

## In one line
How an official guideline, a standard or a product (dietary guidelines, traffic rules, model versions, phone design) kept changing, why it changed, and what to do now.

## Topic signals
- A news hook (a new guideline, a country reversing itself).
- Room for comparison (two countries on one timeline, old vs new versions).

## Narrative skeleton (three finished videos, all 7 paragraphs, 2–3 min)
| Paragraph | Role |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hook + conflict ("the chart is upside down now", "can we still trust it if it keeps changing?") |
| 2 | The starting point: what the first version said |
| 3–5 | Each change: what changed and why (one change per paragraph) |
| 6 | The underlying reason for change (new evidence, a different problem) |
| 7 | What stayed the same + what to do now |

## Required shots / through-line elements
- A year axis (one track per country if several).
- Original text side by side (that version's exact words + highlighter).
- Numbers shown changing with sliders / counters.

## Transition preferences
In the editing-desk video: each paragraph used a different "editing tool" (drag onto the track, beat markers, morph transition, scissors, slider, easing, diff), never the same action twice in a row.

## Fact and platform risks
- Repeat "expected in year X" only as an expert's claim; check who changed first (measured: which of two countries dropped a limit first was the opposite of what people assume).
- Animated transitions must never flash intermediate or out-of-range values.

## Suitable scenes
Validated: info-cards (two videos), editing-desk (one).

## Cases
- Two early videos: 7 paragraphs, info cards.
- One editing desk: two countries on one timeline.
