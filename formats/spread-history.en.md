# How one thing spread (id: spread-history)

中文:[spread-history.md](spread-history.md)

## In one line
How one thing (a food, crop, technology, habit, buzzword) arrived, spread, and what it changed — ending with how to use it today. The author's nutrition channel used it for how foreign crops came to China.

## Topic signals
- A clear "first time" (year + person + original text), a spread route, and a counter-intuitive turn (say, a spice first grown as an ornamental plant when it arrived).
- Knowledge channels need practical value: roughly half history, half practical (the decision-maker's requirement).

## Narrative skeleton (two finished videos, 11–12 paragraphs, 3:00–4:00)
| Paragraph | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Counter-intuitive opening + "treat it as a game / a journey" | "A few hundred years ago this dish didn't have it at all" |
| 2 | First appearance (original text + year) | First mentioned in some old book in some year, one quoted line |
| 3–6 | Spread: places, reasons, who drove it | One region used it to replace something else → it spread along a big river |
| 7 | What it changed | The old order rewritten, a rival pushed out |
| 8–9 | Science / nutrition | What it does in the body; how to choose and eat it today |
| 10–11 | Research evidence (say clearly it's only correlation) | Its association with some health outcome |
| Last | How to eat it (screenshot-able tips) | How to eat or cook it better |

## Required shots / through-line elements
- **One map** runs through the whole video: where the thing travels, what lights up (both videos used a fog reveal).
- A "moment in history" card carries the primary text (vertical woodblock print + red-ink marks).
- Counters / a resource bar make "spread" quantifiable.

## Transition preferences
Pan the map to the next place; each paragraph unlocks a new mechanic (no repeats).

## Fact and platform risks
- Maps are schematic: label provinces only, no administrative or national borders.
- Family records and legends are reported as such ("according to his descendants").
- Tell apart things with the same name (plants with the same name in old texts are often different species); check big numbers like "fed hundreds of millions" against the original research (measured: a widely repeated number was corrected by the primary study).

## Suitable scenes
Validated: strategy-game (two finished videos, well received). Worth trying: case-file with old maps, landscape-scroll (a horizontal journey).
